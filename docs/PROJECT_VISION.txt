ORIGINAL PROJECT BRIEF — LONG-TERM VISION, NOT IMPLEMENTED CAPABILITY
See README.md and docs/SAFETY.md for the v0.1 scope and researched corrections.

I want you to help me design and build a serious long-term aviation research/software project called:

ATC GUARDIAN
AI-Assisted / Highly Automated Air Traffic Control Safety System

PROJECT VISION

I previously trained as a pilot and earned my Private Pilot Certificate.

One problem I noticed while flying general aviation aircraft is that air traffic controllers are human and can become overloaded.

A controller may be handling many aircraft simultaneously and occasionally:

- forget a planned turn
- forget to remind an aircraft about a pending instruction
- issue the wrong altitude
- miss an incorrect pilot readback
- focus on higher-priority traffic and temporarily lose track of a smaller aircraft
- miss developing traffic conflicts
- overlook deteriorating weather
- become overloaded with radio communications
- lose situational awareness during rapidly changing situations

My goal is to explore a future ATC architecture where software maintains continuous awareness of every aircraft and acts as a persistent safety layer.

The long-term concept is NOT simply:

"AI replaces air traffic controllers."

Instead:

AUTOMATION HANDLES REPETITIVE MONITORING
+
DETERMINISTIC SOFTWARE HANDLES SAFETY RULES
+
AI ASSISTS WITH INTERPRETATION AND COMMUNICATION
+
HUMAN CONTROLLERS SUPERVISE THE SYSTEM
+
HUMANS CAN IMMEDIATELY TAKE CONTROL WHEN NEEDED

Human controllers could eventually work more like highly trained supervisors overseeing automated sectors.

Routine aircraft communications could eventually be generated automatically.

However:

- controllers remain present
- controllers monitor the displays
- controllers can intervene instantly
- pilots can request human ATC communication at any time
- emergencies automatically prioritize human involvement
- automation can be disabled immediately

This system must initially exist ONLY as simulation/research software.

It is NOT operational ATC software.

52. ETHICAL DESIGN
==================================================

Automation should not create a two-tier safety system where small general aviation aircraft receive less attention.

Every aircraft should retain persistent digital tracking regardless of size or airline status.

One motivation for this project is improving continuous awareness of aircraft that may otherwise receive less controller attention during high workload.

==================================================
53. ADOPTION STRATEGY
==================================================

The system should be designed around gradual trust.

EARLY STAGE:

Humans control aircraft.

System observes.

NEXT:

System recommends.

NEXT:

System generates instructions humans approve.

NEXT:

Routine transmissions automated under supervision.

NEXT:

Automation handles larger portions of normal traffic while humans oversee the system.

At every stage:

pilot can request human

controller can take over

system can revert to manual

emergencies escalate

automation can be disabled

This allows pilots, controllers, regulators, and the public to develop trust gradually instead of attempting immediate full automation.
